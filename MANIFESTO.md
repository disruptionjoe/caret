# MANIFESTO

Systems fail upstream.

Not at the model layer. Not at inference. At the instruction layer, where humans write sloppy directives and call it prompting. At the context layer, where teams dump everything into the window and hope relevance sorts itself out. At the abstraction layer, where three frameworks rename the same concept and call it progress.

The models got good enough. The instructions did not keep up.

Caret is a portable directive notation for agents. It exists because language is the actual control surface of every agent system, and almost nobody treats it that way.

## The Problem

Most agent failures are language failures.

Ambiguous directives. Redundant context. Definitions that drift between files. Workflows described in prose so loose the parser has to guess intent on every pass.

The industry response has been to add more. More tokens. More context. More abstraction layers. More ceremony around the same underlying weakness: imprecise language given to systems that require precision.

Context is not a landfill. Stop treating it like one.

If three products use three different terms for the same underlying idea, that is not innovation. It is branding trying to outrun clarity.

## What Caret Does

Caret gives you a notation. Compact, portable, parseable.

One term means one thing. Directives are explicit. Context is chosen, not dumped. Structure is enforced at the syntax level so ambiguity cannot hide in formatting.

The result: instructions that travel between tools, agents, and workflows without losing intent. Systems that can be read, reasoned about, and modified without a priesthood of interpreters.

The parser should not have to guess what you meant. Neither should your agent. Neither should your team.

## What Caret Believes

Clear language produces better systems. Not prettier systems. Better ones.

Every loose term creates hidden cost. Usually in tokens. Always in judgment. Vagueness does not become sophisticated just because it appears inside an agent stack.

Ambiguity is how bad systems pretend to be flexible.

## What Caret Rejects

The idea that prompting should remain half craft, half folklore. That context management should be improvised. That writing for agents should stay soft, hand-wavy, and loaded with duplicate concepts dressed up as insight.

Agents do not need more vibes. They need cleaner instructions and fewer opportunities for human imprecision to masquerade as depth.

## Why It Matters

A notation is a small thing. Small things travel. Small things repeat. Small things become habits, and habits become systems.

If the base layer of instruction is weak, the rest of the stack inherits the weakness and gives it nicer packaging.

One term should mean one thing. Context should be chosen, not dumped. Technical writing should leave very little to interpret and nothing to decorate. A good system should be easier to read than to misunderstand.

Good notation reduces friction without begging for credit. It sharpens execution without becoming the story. It disappears into the work and improves the work anyway.

Not more ceremony. Not more fog. Not another cathedral around a paperclip.

Just cleaner instructions. Better context discipline. Less waste.

Language sharp enough to carry intent without dropping half of it on the floor.

That is Caret.
